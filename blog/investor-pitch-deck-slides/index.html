The 10 Slides Every Investor Pitch Deck Needs

2026-10-283 min read

Seed-stage investors spend two to four minutes on a first-pass deck. Yours has to communicate the whole business in that window. The good news: the winning structure has been stable for a decade, and it is ten slides.

Slide 1: Title and one-line pitch

Company name, logo, and a single sentence describing what you do and for whom. "X helps Y do Z" is enough. If your one-liner needs a comma, it needs another edit.

Slide 2: The problem

Describe a problem that is frequent, painful, and expensive — ideally one your audience has personally experienced. Quantify it if you can: hours lost, dollars wasted, risk created.

Slide 3: Your solution

Show, do not tell. One screenshot or mockup of the product doing its core job beats three bullets of adjectives. If you have a demo video under thirty seconds, link it here.

Slide 4: Why now

The most-skipped slide and the one that most often decides the meeting. What changed — technology, regulation, behavior — that makes this possible today but was not possible two years ago?

Slide 5: Market size

Use the standard three-layer view: total market, serviceable market, and the slice you can realistically win in three years. Bottom-up math (number of target customers × realistic price) reads better than top-down analyst figures.

Slide 6: The product

Two or three screenshots maximum. Highlight the one workflow that makes customers switch. If you have early usage data — retention, activation, weekly actives — this is the place for it.

Slide 7: Business model

How you charge, how much, and the unit economics that matter: gross margin, CAC, LTV. One simple table beats a financial model dump.

Slide 8: Traction

The growth chart is the most important chart in the deck. Pick the single metric that best represents momentum — revenue, users, or engagement — and show it as a clean line going up and to the right. Logos of customers or pilots belong here too.

Slide 9: Competition

A two-by-two positioning map or a compact comparison table. The message investors want: you understand the landscape and have a durable reason to win it. Never claim you have no competitors.

Slide 10: Team and the ask

Why you — founders' relevant wins in two lines each. Then the ask: how much you are raising, and what milestones it buys you. Concrete milestones ("18 months of runway to reach $1M ARR") are more credible than vague ones.

Final checklist before you send

  1. Every headline reads as a takeaway sentence
  2. One idea per slide, ten slides total (appendix does not count)
  3. Every number has a source and a date
  4. File size under 10 MB so it opens on a phone
  5. The ask appears twice: slide 10 and your email body
