An annual report has two audiences and one chance. Stakeholders skim it for the numbers; employees and partners read it for the story. The reports that satisfy both follow a predictable arc: what we said we would do, what we did, what it earned, what we will do next. Here is how to build each section.

## Open with the year in one page

The first content slide is the whole report compressed: three to five headline achievements, each with a number. "Revenue up 24% to $8.2M", "Launched in two new markets", "Churn down from 3.1% to 2.2%". If a reader stops here, they should still be able to repeat your year in one sentence.

Write these as achievements, not activities. "Held 12 board meetings" is an activity; "Completed the Series B and extended runway to 30 months" is an achievement.

## Financial performance: trends, not snapshots

The financial section fails when it shows this year alone. Every number needs its trend:

- Revenue — three-year line, with the driver behind the change called out in one sentence
- Costs — split into the two or three buckets leadership controls, not a 40-line expense table
- Margins and cash — where efficiency improved or leaked
- One unit-economics number the business is actually managed on (ARR per customer, cost per case, margin per unit)

Round ruthlessly. A report full of cents looks precise but reads slow; $8.2M is more digestible than $8,234,117 and equally credible at this altitude.

## Operations: what the year actually contained

This is where non-financial work gets its due, and where most reports go flat. Structure it by the initiatives that consumed the year, not by department:

1. What was built or launched, and the result it produced
2. What changed in how the company operates — systems, process, team
3. What was tried and did not work, and what it taught

Including the third point is what separates a credible report from a brochure. One honest paragraph about a missed bet buys more trust than ten polished wins.

## People and milestones

A short section: headcount at year end versus start, key hires, and two or three milestones that mark the company's stage — certifications, partnerships, products shipped, markets entered. If your industry has compliance or ESG reporting duties, the summary line belongs here with a link to the full document.

## The outlook: next year in three commitments

Close with the coming year expressed as three to five measurable commitments. Not a strategy dump — three things the reader should hold you to. "Open the Singapore office and reach $1M ARR there", "Ship the enterprise tier", "Hold churn under 2%". Next year's report will be measured against this slide, which is exactly the discipline that makes it useful.

## Before you send it

- Read only the headlines in order — the story should survive with all body text hidden
- Check every number against the source document, then freeze the figures; late edits are where errors breed
- Get one reader from outside the team to confirm the three commitments on the final slide are the same three leadership would name
