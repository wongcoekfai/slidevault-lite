What to Include in a Marketing Report (With Free Template)

2026-10-283 min read

A marketing report has one job: help the reader decide what to do next. Everything that does not serve that job — vanity metrics, raw exports, feature lists — is noise. Here is the structure that works for monthly and quarterly reviews.

Start with the executive summary

One slide, written last, containing exactly three things:

  1. The headline result — "Leads grew 18% QoQ, driven by paid search"
  2. The one surprise — good or bad, the thing leadership will ask about anyway
  3. The single recommendation — what you need approved, changed, or funded

If the reader stops after this slide, they should still make the right decision.

The KPI dashboard: five metrics, no more

Choose the metrics that map to business outcomes, not channel activity:

  • Pipeline or revenue attributed to marketing — the number that buys your budget
  • Cost per lead / per acquisition — efficiency over time, not just this period
  • Conversion rate at the one or two funnel steps you actually influence
  • Traffic, split by new vs. returning
  • One channel-specific north star — email CTR, organic impressions, whatever you most moved this cycle

Show each as a trend line with the target marked, not as a lonely big number.

Channel breakdown: one slide per channel

For each channel answer the same three questions in the same order: what did we spend, what did it produce, what changes next period. Consistency across channels is what makes reports comparable month over month.

Cover the channels that matter to you — typically paid search, paid social, organic/SEO, email, and content. Kill channels from the report (not from the plan) if they stayed flat and boring for two cycles.

Insights, not observations

The difference between a junior report and a senior one:

  • Observation: "Email open rate fell from 32% to 27%."
  • Insight: "Open rate fell 5 points after we moved sends to 8 AM; subject-line A/B tests show morning audiences respond to outcome-based titles — we will test two variants next cycle."

Every insight names a cause and a next action. If you cannot name the cause, write the experiment that will find it.

Next period's plan on one slide

Three to five initiatives maximum, each with an owner, an expected result, and a number it should move. This slide is what turns the report from a backward-looking document into a working agreement.

Delivery tips

  • Send the deck 24 hours before the meeting so the meeting is a discussion, not a read-aloud
  • Keep a one-page appendix with definitions of every metric — it prevents the "wait, which conversion rate?" tangent
  • Archive past reports in one shared folder; quarter-over-quarter comparisons are where the real insights live
